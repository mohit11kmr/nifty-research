## Contract Selection

For each observation day `d`, the applicable contract expiry =
`min{e : e > d}` from the day-`d` bhavcopy expiry list — the exact historical
analog of the frozen `next_thursday` model (current week's contract, unless it
expires today). Replaces `next_thursday(d)` in `backtest_frozen.py`. Of the 48
Phase F2 trades, **45 changed contract expiry vs Phase F**; contract weekday
distribution: 42 Tuesday, 3 Thursday, 3 Monday.

Entry is unchanged except: the contract is the historically applicable one,
and its LTP mark comes from that specific `(expiry, strike, side)` row. BS
fallback uses the contract's TRUE time-to-expiry.

**Contract validation** (new, model-only): a candidate is traded only if
`(expiry, strike, side)` exists in the day-`d` chain. Otherwise
`CONTRACT_UNAVAILABLE` (no trade, no silent substitution, no fabricated
price). Result: 0 of 48 F2 candidates were unavailable.

## Square-Off

`simulate_trade` still squares off at `j == expiry` (the corrected actual
expiry), at the frozen configured time semantics (day-level close mark). No
invented exit time, no auto-roll, no post-expiry holding (test-verified).

## Leakage Controls

The expiry mapping uses only the expiry list published/listed on day `d`
itself. Contract expiry dates are exchange-listed weeks in advance and were
knowable at the decision timestamp. The mapping is a fixed, deterministic,
hashed artifact — no future contract outcome is inspected. Every generated
`expiry > entry date` (asserted in tests).

### What changed because of the expiry correction (F → F2)

The corrected contracts expire sooner and on the true weekday:
- Average hold 3.4 → 2.4 days; expiry square-offs 26 → 18; stops 15 → 18;
  targets 7 → 12. Shorter true time-to-expiry pushes more positions through
  stop/target before the (wrong) Thursday, cutting the "expiry bailout" tail.
- Net +3,567 → +1,906 (~-47%); PF unchanged at ~1.01; win rate 43.8 → 33.3%.
- Max drawdown improved ( -103.7k → -51.7k ). Grade A net flipped negative;
  A+ turned positive. Regime distribution of trades is unchanged (same
  candidates); only contract timing/exit changed.

**Interpretation: the near-breakeven Phase F result does NOT materially
improve under the correct historical contract model — it stays near breakeven
(PF ≈ 1.01), with lower absolute net and lower max drawdown. The expiry
correction changes the payoff shape (fewer expiry bailouts, more stop/target
resolutions), not the edge class.**
